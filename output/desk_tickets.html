Reflection

My answers after the official run, backed by evidence from audit_trail.json and the saved drafts.

1 · How would you evaluate the performance of the agents on each ticket, and why?

The agents were thorough, and Inventory played a good role. I just don't think Customer Service wrote emails that could be used in real life.

TicketCorrect decisionFollowed the rulesEfficiencyCustomer messageWhy
#101 teeStrongStrongOKWeak Inventory found S = 0 and the blocked vendor, then delegated to Accounting on its own to clear invoice 501. That's good initiative. But the email tells Tauhid the shop has an "overdue invoice". A real shop would never tell a customer it hasn't paid its bills.
#102 rentStrongStrongWeakOK The right amount, the right date, and the cash math shown ($160 left). But it took 5 agent runs, with the lease checked 3× and cash 4×, for one rent payment. The landlord note says "pending human approval", which is internal process, not something you'd send to a landlord.
#103 hoodiesStrongStrongOKWeak The best judgment of the run: Accounting tested 10%, 15%, and the 25% floor, and the Boss chose a modest 10%, keeping the margin at 57.85%. The quote names our vendor, says "pending funding", and ends without asking for the order. A student club wouldn't know what to do next.

Overall: the facts and the money handling were reliable every time. No made-up numbers, no payment without me, no negative cash. The weak link was the last step: turning correct facts into a message a customer would actually want to receive. A fix would be a Customer Service rule like "describe delays as supplier delays, never mention our bills or cash", plus a human editing pass before anything is sent.

2 · For each ticket, how did Actual compare to the Expected plan?

They were similar enough. The conditions were controlled, and the agents acted as expected.

  • #101: all 5 checklist items passed: the $840 request, no PO while the vendor was blocked, a draft offering M/L/XL, and awaiting_approval. The one surprise was the routing: Inventory pulled Accounting in itself (Boss → Inventory → Accounting) instead of waiting for the Boss.
  • #102: all 5 passed, including the exact cash math ($3,400 − $840 − $2,400 = $160). Facilities delegated to Accounting itself, as I'd hoped.
  • #103: it matched my expected pick, option A (partial fill now at 10–15% off). One difference: I expected the vendor block and cash to stop the restock. Because I approved invoice 501 before #103 ran, only cash stopped it ($264 needed vs. $160). So the order of human approvals changed the agents' reasoning, and they handled it correctly.
  • The setup was controlled: one desk date, 3 tickets, rules enforced in code. That's why Actual matched Expected so closely. Messier real tickets would test the agents harder.

3 · What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

Accounting and Facilities could probably be merged into one agent that takes care of every expense and cost. This separation only made things more complicated.

Why the evidence agrees: Facilities only had 3 read-only tools, and Accounting already had check_lease_and_cash. Facilities couldn't pay rent itself, so on #102 it always had to hand off to Accounting. The result was 5 agent runs and repeated lookups (lease 3×, cash 4×) for a single $2,400 bill.

A single Finance & Facilities agent would hold the lease, cash, invoice, and margin tools plus the approval-request tools. It would handle any money-out ticket in one loop, with fewer model calls and tokens, and fewer places for the numbers to drift. The human-approval guardrail stays exactly the same, because it lives in the MCP tools, not in how the agents are split.

4 · Three new problems this team could solve with the tools we built

Paying utility bills

Electricity, internet, or water bills entered as vendor invoices go through the same path as invoice 501. Accounting checks the due date and days overdue, checks cash, and files request_payment_approval. A human approves, and the payment is recorded.

Uses: check_vendor_and_invoices · check_cash_position · request_payment_approval · approve_request

Handling online orders

An online order is the same shape as ticket #101. Inventory checks the SKU and size and suggests other sizes, Accounting finds and unblocks the restock vendor, and Customer Service drafts the order confirmation or the "we're out of your size" note.

Uses: get_ticket · check_stock_and_pricing · request_purchase_order · save_customer_draft. Note: actually shipping an order would need a small "reduce inventory" tool.

Building income statements

The agents can already pull costs (unit cost, list price, margins), every payment made (rent, invoices, restocks), and cash on hand. That's the expense side of an income statement, plus gross margin per product.

Uses: check_cash_position · check_discount_margin · check_stock_and_pricing. Note: no revenue is modeled yet, so the sales line would need a sales table.

5 · Three new problems the team could not solve, and what we'd need

Defects and returns

We have no way to take a product back. Nothing puts stock back on the shelf, refunds a customer, or charges a defect back to the vendor.

New tools: log_return(order, sku, size, reason), restock_item, request_refund_approval (money out, so a human approves), file_vendor_defect_claim.

New agent: Returns & Quality. It checks the return policy (custom items can't be returned), inspects the reason, and talks to Inventory and Finance.

Paying taxes

Nothing tracks sales tax collected, what's owed to the state, or filing deadlines, and the payment tool only accepts invoices and rent.

New tools: calculate_sales_tax(period), tax_calendar (due dates), request_tax_payment_approval, plus a sales/revenue table to calculate from.

New agent: Tax & Compliance, with a mandatory human (or CPA) review. A wrong tax filing is far costlier than a late tee.

Paying employees and tracking their schedules

There are no employee, shift, or hours data, and nothing to calculate or pay wages.

New tools: get_schedule, log_hours, calculate_payroll(period), request_payroll_approval (money out, so a human approves), draft_schedule_change.

New agent: People & Payroll, with privacy guardrails: wages and personal data are only visible to this agent, never written into the audit trail.

The pattern is the same as everything we built: agents can read and request, but only a human can move money. Each new capability just needs its own tools, and its money-out step routed through the approval queue.
